# Frameworks and Section Guidance

## Contents
- PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)
- AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
- StoryBrand
- Section guidance (hero through final CTA)

## PAS

Best when the reader already feels the pain (search traffic, referrals from peers with the same problem).

1. **Problem** - name it in the reader's words, as specifically as possible ("Friday payroll takes all afternoon").
2. **Agitate** - the cost of leaving it alone: money, time, risk, embarrassment. Use concrete scenarios, not adjectives.
3. **Solution** - the product as the direct answer, leading with the mechanism.

Risk: over-agitating reads as fearmongering. One or two sharp consequences beat a paragraph of doom.

## AIDA

Best for cold traffic (paid social, display) and comparison shoppers.

1. **Attention** - a specific, surprising claim or outcome.
2. **Interest** - how it works, in two or three sentences.
3. **Desire** - benefits and proof that make the reader picture the result.
4. **Action** - the CTA with friction reducers.

## StoryBrand

Best for services, brand-led products, and founders selling expertise. The customer is the hero, the brand is the guide.

1. Hero (customer) wants something
2. Has a problem (external, internal, philosophical)
3. Meets a guide with empathy and authority
4. Who gives a plan (3 steps)
5. And calls them to action (direct CTA plus a lower-commitment transitional CTA, such as a guide download)
6. That helps them avoid failure
7. And ends in success

## Section guidance

**Hero**
- Headline: the outcome, ideally under 10 words.
- Subhead: the mechanism, one or two sentences.
- Primary CTA plus a friction reducer beneath it.
- One proof element if real (a metric, a recognizable customer, a rating).

**Problem**
- Two or three specific scenarios the reader recognizes.
- Name the current workaround and why it fails.

**Solution / features**
- Three to five blocks. Each: benefit headline, one-sentence explanation, the feature that delivers it.
- Order by what the reader cares about most, not by build order.

**How it works**
- Three or four steps, each starting with a verb. The first step should look easy.
- End with the CTA.

**Social proof**
- Testimonials: quote + name + role + company, with permission. A quote with a number beats a quote with praise.
- Case study snippet: before, after, timeframe.
- Only real logos, ratings, and counts. Placeholders otherwise.

**Pricing** (if on the page)
- Highlight one recommended plan and say who it is for.
- Put the guarantee or cancellation terms next to the price, where the hesitation happens.

**FAQ**
- Five to seven real objections: price, time to value, switching effort, security and data, fit for edge cases, contract terms.
- Answer in two or three sentences, confidently, without hedging.

**Final CTA**
- Restate the promise in one line.
- Risk reversal (trial, guarantee, cancel anytime) if true.
- Same action as the hero CTA.
